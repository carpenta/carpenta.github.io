# Fairway Note — golf score notes

## Contract

Static GitHub Pages application for recording 18-hole golf rounds on a phone, one at a time, and keeping the finished ones on the device. Each hole counts swings per club (drives, approaches, putts) with one tap each, plus OB and hazard penalties and mulligans. Totals and a round analysis update on every tap.

- Hole strokes = drives + approaches + putts + OBs + hazards. Each OB and each hazard (penalty area drop) adds one penalty stroke; a replayed shot is counted as another drive or approach. Mulligans are shown but never counted.
- The hole being played counts toward totals and analysis only once it has a putt, so a tee shot alone never reads as an eagle. A hole finished without a putt (chip-in) counts once the player moves to another hole.
- A round is played on a chosen course: a bundled course with a front and a back nine, or a course typed in by hand. Pars start from the course's own published scorecard (all par 4 for a typed course) and can be set to 3, 4 or 5 per hole from the scorecard. The default is Everis Golf Resort, Saebyul nine (holes 1–9) then Lake nine (holes 10–18). Rounds saved before courses could be chosen have no `course` field; they are shown as Everis Saebyul·Lake and keep their saved pars.
- The course picker comes first on open: with nothing saved, with a saved round that has nothing recorded (older versions saved an untouched round on open), and with a round from an earlier day. For an earlier-day round its back button reads "지난 라운드 이어서" and continues that round; starting a new course from it asks before the earlier round is replaced (a round with a finished hole is kept among past rounds first), and closing it leaves the earlier-day banner. It also opens on "새 라운드 시작" (after its confirmation). The last choice is preselected, so starting is one tap; cancelling a new round keeps the current one. The choice is remembered in `fairway-note:prefs:v1` (course id and nine ids, or a typed name); losing it never affects the round.
- The round is saved to one localStorage key (`fairway-note:round:v1`) so it survives reloads, and is replaced when a new round starts, after a round with a finished hole has been moved to the past rounds (see Past rounds and backup). Opening the page writes nothing; choosing a course or the first edit saves the round and asks once for persistent storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`, failures ignored). A stored value this version cannot read (broken, or written by a newer version) is never overwritten or removed: it is first copied to `fairway-note:corrupt:<timestamp>` (newest 3 kept), and if that copy fails the round is not saved. Nothing is sent over the network; there is no account, analytics, sessionStorage, IndexedDB or cookie. Cache Storage holds only the app's own built files for offline use (see Offline). A CSP meta tag sets `connect-src 'none'` and limits scripts, styles and images to the same origin with no inline style elements (bar widths are set through the CSSOM, which CSP allows).
- Each hole stores `hazard` next to the other counts. Rounds saved before it existed have no field and read as 0, with nothing copied aside; a value that is present but not a count from 0 to 15 makes the round unreadable, as for any counter. The round stays `version: 1`. An older build of the app ignores the field: it shows such a hole without its hazard strokes and drops them when it saves, as it does with shot tags.
- Results leave the device only when the player asks: "이메일로 보내기" is a `mailto:` link with the subject and a plain-text report (totals, finished holes, analysis) and no recipient, which hands the text to the phone's mail app; "공유하기" calls the Web Share API, falling back to copying the report to the clipboard. The app itself makes no request.
- If storage is unavailable, the app keeps working in memory and says so on the entry screen.
- Saved rounds carry `lastEdited` (ms). A round whose last edit is at least 12 hours old (or, for rounds saved without `lastEdited`, one from an earlier date) is offered to continue or replace on load; replacing asks for confirmation, and continuing saves the round so the offer does not return. A round that crosses midnight is not offered. A save from another tab replaces the open tab's round through the `storage` event, so tabs do not silently overwrite each other. If another tab removes the saved round, the open tab keeps its round in memory and says it will be saved again on the next edit.
- After the first visit the app opens and records without a connection (see Offline).
- Optional shot details (see Shot details). A + tap records the stroke on its own; details never block or slow entry.
- Finished rounds are kept in a second key, `fairway-note:history:v1`, with trends and a read-only view of each. They move between devices only in a backup file that the player saves or shares; the app never uploads it, and importing one never replaces the round being recorded without the player's yes (see Past rounds and backup).

Out of scope: playing partners, GPS distances, other languages, putt distances, automatic sync between devices.

## Course data

- `src/data/courses.json` is committed and bundled; nothing is fetched at runtime. Each course has `id`, `name` (Korean; `en` is null), `area` (picker chip), `region` (province and city), `source` (the official page the pars come from), `checked` (date) and `nines` (`id`, `name`, nine pars of 3, 4 or 5, and optional `holes`).
- `scripts/build-courses.mjs <research-dir>` builds it from local research notes that are not committed. A nine is bundled only when it is marked high confidence and at least one source is the course's own website (same host) or its operator's site. Values found only on commercial or crowd-sourced services whose terms forbid reuse (Hole19, GolfPass, 18Birdies, GolfingRecord, Golfzon, SmartScore, Kakao) are excluded, and so are values from GolfZon County's own site, whose terms (article 16) reserve copyright and forbid redistribution without consent; public data lists (data.go.kr) are used only to confirm the course list and hole counts, as they carry no hole pars; nothing derived from OpenStreetMap (coordinates, English names matched from it) is copied, so the bundle carries no ODbL obligations. Nines with a par outside 3–5 are skipped and reported. The script checks unique ids, nine pars per nine and that Everis has Saebyul, Lake and Pine.
- Hole notes: a nine may carry `holes`, nine entries in hole order, each `null` or a `HoleInfo` (`distances`, `layout`, `guideUrl`, `si`; see `src/courses.ts`). The script merges them from local per-nine hole research, and from the hole fields of the official Gyeongsang research (whose nines cite only the course's own pages), under the same rule as the pars: the page the notes were read from must be on the course's own or its operator's site, and a link is kept only as an https page on that site.
  - Lengths need a stated unit (m or yd): one per named tee (metres when both are published), or the single length a course publishes with no tee name (no `tee`). The tee most players start from (white, else regular, blue or front) comes first, with both its greens on a two-green hole (A/B or left/right, each tee's greens kept together); other tees follow a fixed order, and tees a course names only by number come highest first.
  - Dropped: lengths outside the par's range (par 3 60–280 m, par 4 180–560 m, par 5 300–650 m), holes whose tees all show one length (placeholders), every length of a course whose pages do not state the unit clearly (Cypress gives the same numbers in m and yd; Ansung's card prints none), and every length of a Gyeongsang nine whose research notes say its own pages disagree about lengths (totals that do not add up, tees out of order, m and yd that do not convert).
  - Stroke indexes are kept as published when the nine has 1–9 once each, or 1–18 once each together with another nine of the course; otherwise the nine gets none.
  - A layout note keeps at most three short phrases within 40 characters, a dogleg phrase first so the drawn hole bends; the phrases are short descriptions written from the guide, never copied guide text.
  - Everything left out is counted by reason in the script's output.
- At runtime `holeInfo()` finds a round's hole notes by the nine names the round keeps, keeps a tee-less length and a handicap index from 1 to 18, and leaves out a link that is not https, an empty note and a length that is not positive.
- A round stores its course by display name (`{ id, name, front, back }`), so a saved round reads the same if the bundle changes. A round has 18 holes, or 9 for one nine played on its own (v11): then `back` is empty, and a 9-hole round with a back nine named or with no course is unreadable (a truncated 18-hole round stays broken). The choice may carry `holes: 9` in the prefs key. A course or nine that is no longer bundled falls back to the default course or that course's first nines when the remembered choice is offered again.
- No hole images are copied from course sites or apps. The hole is drawn by the app (see UI) and the official guide is linked instead.

## Architecture

TypeScript + Vite, no runtime dependencies, no UI framework.

- `src/round.ts` — pure model: the `Round`, `Hole` and `RoundCourse` types, the default course and course label, counter bounds (0–15), strokes, played (a hole with at least one stroke or penalty), green in regulation, totals and `analyze()`.
- `src/courses.ts` — the bundled course list, area chips, search by name, Korean initial consonants (초성) or region, the pars a choice starts with, and the optional hole notes for a round's hole.
- `src/course-art.ts` — the hole drawn behind the screen: a shape by par and hole (and a dogleg bend read from the layout note) as numbers, and the decorative SVG made from them.
- `src/version.ts` — the app version shown in the tab bar and the menu; each improvement round adds one (`docs/IMPROVEMENTS.md`).
- `src/picker.ts` — the course picker, a modal `<dialog>`.
- `src/report.ts` — the plain-text report (named after the round's course), its subject and the `mailto:` link, plus the analysis notes shown on the page.
- `src/icons.ts` — static SVG icons for the three clubs (driver head, iron, putter).
- `src/storage.ts` — validates and restores a saved round and the remembered course choice. Any malformed round (wrong version, hole count, par, count or course) is ignored and a new round starts, after the value is copied aside. Malformed shot details alone do not reject the round: they are dropped, the counts are kept, and the original value is copied aside before the first save. Storage exceptions are caught.
- `src/offline.ts` — registers the service worker and shows the update prompt.
- `scripts/service-worker.ts` — builds `dist/sw.js`; a small plugin in `vite.config.ts` calls it after the build.
- `src/history.ts` — past rounds: the history key, reading every entry with the same checks as the round key, copying an unreadable history aside, keeping a round once, merging, ordering and trends.
- `src/backup.ts` — the backup file: building it, reading and checking it, and planning an import.
- `src/history-view.ts` — `renderHistory(container, page)` mounts the past rounds view (trends, list, status) into any container and builds the dialog for one round on first use; `keepBeforeNewRound()` and `newRoundQuestion()` serve every way into a new round. It renders when the history changes, never on a tap.
- `src/backup-view.ts` — `renderBackup(container, page)` mounts the backup controls (save, share, import, status) into any container.
- `src/dom.ts` — the small element builder and text helpers shared by the page and both views.
- `src/main.ts` — the two views, the hole panel, the counters and stakes, the sheets, the scorecard and analysis, rendered from the current round after every change with DOM nodes rather than HTML strings (the icons and the drawn hole are static markup). It lends its scorecard (read-only columns, no hole buttons), analysis and share code to both views through one `Page` object, so a past round reads like the current one, and mounts them into `#history` and `#backup`.

Analysis covers played holes only:
- putts per hole, one-putts, three-putts or worse
- greens in regulation: at least one putt after drives + approaches + OBs + hazards ≤ par − 2
- score distribution, average per par, best and worst hole (omitted when every hole has the same score against par)
- strokes lost per area against a regulation pattern of one tee shot, par − 3 approaches and two putts, plus penalty strokes (OB and hazard). Each hole adds only its own excess, so a better-than-regulation hole never offsets a loss elsewhere. Every area tied for the most lost strokes is named.

## Shot details

- Drives, approaches and putts can carry one tag per shot. Tags per counter: drive `fairway left right ob-left ob-right whiff mishit`, approach `green short long left right bunker hazard whiff`, putt `in short long left right`. Visible labels are Korean (`TAG_LABELS` in `src/round.ts`).
- Storage: optional `tags` on a hole, `{ driver?, approach?, putts? }`, each an array aligned with that counter's shots (entry i is shot i), never longer than the count and without trailing nulls; a hole with no tags has no field. `obByTag` counts the OB penalties added by OB tags. The round stays `version: 1`: older rounds read unchanged. An older build of the app reads newer rounds but drops the tags when it saves.
- An OB tag adds one OB penalty only if the OB counter does not already cover every OB-tagged drive, so an OB counted by hand is never counted twice; switching left/right OB changes nothing. Clearing an OB tag removes a penalty only if a tag added one (`obByTag`), so tag-then-clear is an exact undo. Minus on a counter drops its last shot's tag and any penalty that tag added. Minus on OB keeps `obByTag` no larger than the OB count. The approach tag `hazard` is a note only; the hazard stake is what adds the penalty.
- UI: in 상세 the tee-shot pad and 미스샷 row write the first drive's tag (drives also accept far-left, far-right, long and short). Tags on other shots, from earlier versions, are kept, analysed and reported. A per-shot list on tapping a count comes later.
- Analysis and report: `analyze()` adds tag counts over finished holes (`details`, null without tags). The scorecard view and the text report show a "샷 세부" section only when tags exist: counts per counter, drive direction left/right with OB, a note when putts were mostly short or mostly long, and the number of whiffs. Report hole lines append that hole's tags.

## Past rounds and backup

- Storage: `fairway-note:history:v1` holds a JSON array of `{ id, endedAt, round }` in the order rounds were kept. `id` is a random UUID (time plus random digits where `crypto.randomUUID` is unavailable) and survives export and import; `endedAt` is ms since the epoch; `round` is read with the same checks as the round key (hazard counts and shot details included). An entry needs at least one finished hole. The key appears with the first kept round and is removed with the last.
- Unreadable values: a stored history that is not a JSON array, or has an entry that fails the checks, repeats an id, carries unknown fields or loses shot details when read, is copied to `fairway-note:corrupt:<timestamp>` (the round key's slots: newest 3 kept, the same value never twice) on load and before every write. If the copy fails, the history is not written and the section says so.
- Keeping a round: every way into a new round ends in `startRound` once a course is chosen, and that is where a round with a finished hole is added to the history, just before it is replaced; cancelling the picker or going back from it ("지난 라운드 이어서") keeps nothing. "새 라운드 시작" and the earlier-day banner ask "지금 라운드를 지난 라운드에 보관하고 새로 시작할까요?"; the picker that opens first for an earlier-day round asks the same about "<date> 라운드". A round with no finished hole is not kept, and the question says so. A round already kept (same date and the same par, counts, tags and OB link on every hole, whatever the key order or the hole being shown) is never added again, so a crash between keeping and clearing cannot duplicate it. If the history cannot be written, a second question asks whether to start anyway; only that yes loses the round.
- List and trends: newest first by round date, then by when kept. Each row shows the date, the course as the page labels it, strokes and score against par, finished holes, putts and OB. Trends use full 18-hole rounds only, so a short round never lowers the average: rounds kept (and how many are full), average strokes and against par, the latest 5 once there are more than 5, putts and OB per round, greens in regulation as a share of holes, and a line of the latest 10 rounds' strokes. The line is an inline SVG built with DOM APIs, with its values in an `aria-label`.
- One round: a modal `<dialog>` the size of the viewport (at most 640px wide) with a sticky title bar and close button, built by the view on first use and appended to `<body>`. It shows the page's scorecard columns read-only (no hole or par buttons) with the total line, the analysis, the round's `mailto:` report and share or copy, and deletes after a confirm. The close button and Escape close it; the page behind does not scroll while it is open.
- Backup file: `fairway-note-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json` (local date) holding `{ app: "fairway-note", format: 1, exportedAt, current, history }`. `exportedAt` is ISO 8601; `current` is the round being recorded, or null when nothing is entered; `history` is the stored entries. It is saved through a Blob URL and `a[download]`. Where `navigator.canShare({ files })` accepts a JSON file, a second button hands the same file to the Web Share API. Both work offline and make no request; the CSP is unchanged.
- Import: `<input type="file" accept="application/json,.json">`; files over 5 MB are refused before reading. A file that is not JSON, not `app: "fairway-note"`, or not `format: 1` with a `history` array changes nothing and says so; a higher format asks for a reload to get the newer app. Every entry and the `current` round get the same checks as stored ones, and failures are counted. Entries join the history unless their id or the same round is already there. The backup's round in progress, if new here, replaces the round being recorded only after a confirm, which first keeps the current round when it has a finished hole; declining files it among past rounds instead. The status line reports imported, skipped and unreadable counts.
- Right after a round is kept from the summary, 지난 라운드 shows a backup reminder card (v10): "라운드를 보관했어요", one sentence on why, "백업 파일 저장" (the backup view's own download) and "나중에"; after saving it reads "백업 파일을 저장했어요" with 닫기. It is held in memory only and hides the home-screen card while it shows.
- Once a round is kept, 지난 라운드 shows a one-line home-screen card (v9) between the "지금 라운드" card and the list: "방법 보기" expands why (browsers may clear sites not opened for a while) and the iPhone and Android steps; "알겠어요" puts it away for good (`home: true` in the prefs key). It is not shown when opened from the home screen (`display-mode: standalone`), and nothing pops up on 기록.
- Where they are: the list mounts in the 지난 라운드 tab under a "지금 라운드" card (the round being recorded: course, date, holes recorded, score, 이어서 기록), and the backup controls in the menu's 백업과 복원 panel. Each view only needs a container, so it moved there unchanged. Rows and buttons are at least 44px tall, and the list, the dialog and the menu fit 360px without horizontal scrolling and 375×553 with the dialog filling the screen.

## Offline

- `dist/sw.js` is generated at build time with the list of every built file (`index.html`, `assets/*`, icons, manifest; not itself, source maps or dot files) and a build ID, the first 12 hex digits of a SHA-256 over the file names and contents. All paths are relative to the worker, so it works at `/tools/fairway-note/` and its scope is that folder.
- The page registers it with `new URL('./sw.js', document.baseURI)` in production builds only, when the browser supports service workers. A failed registration is ignored and the app keeps working online.
- Install fetches every listed file with `cache: 'reload'`, bypassing the HTTP cache (GitHub Pages serves `max-age=600`), into the cache `fairway-note-<build ID>`.
- Fetch is cache-first for same-origin GETs of precached files and for navigations to the tool's folder or its `index.html` (any query string), which get the cached `index.html`. A miss falls back to the network. Every other request is left to the browser.
- The worker never calls `skipWaiting` on its own. When a new worker is waiting and the page already has a controller, a small fixed button "새 버전이 있습니다 · 다시 열기" appears above the bottom bars without blocking the page. Tapping it posts `SKIP_WAITING`; the page reloads on `controllerchange`. The round lives in localStorage, so reloading keeps it.
- Activate deletes only older `fairway-note-` caches; caches of other tools on the same origin are left alone.
- The CSP is unchanged: `default-src 'self'` already allows a same-origin worker, and the worker's own fetches are not page connections.

## UI

- Mobile first, light theme for outdoor reading, system fonts, `word-break: keep-all` for Korean. Colours are grass tones: Turf `#2F7D3A` (brand, primary button, selected states), Fairway `#5BAA4F`, Rough `#3E6B2E` and Putting green `#8CCB6E` (the drawn hole), Turf ink `#17301A` (text), scorecard paper `#F3F7EF` (page), under-par red `#C0392B` (marks under par, the hazard stake) and sand `#D9B45A` (focus ring, bogey bars). The app icon, manifest and `theme-color` keep their earlier green for now.
- Three views (v6; see `docs/IMPROVEMENTS.md` and `design/v6.html`). **기록** shows only the hole being played; **스코어카드** shows the whole round; **지난 라운드** shows this round and the kept ones. A dark deep-turf bottom tab bar (스코어카드, 기록 in the centre, 지난 라운드, the selected one on a soft pill) switches them on every screen width; it is a `tablist` with roving tabindex (arrow keys that go round, Home, End).
